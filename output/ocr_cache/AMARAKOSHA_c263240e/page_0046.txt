MALLINATHA’s AMARAPADAPARIJATA

Mallinatha, the author of the Amarapadaparijata, the second
comm. given in this edition is to be distinguished from his
well-known namesake, the commentator on the pajicsamahé-
kavya-s. This commentator on the Ak. too hails from the
Andhra country as testified by his liberal use of Telugu
equivalents in all the three Kanda-s. His father was Nrsimha-
sarin! of the Srivatsagotra. He also gives his family name as
Bollatinmi:

बोछारिम्मिदसिंहसर्तिनयः श्रीमदछिनाथो . . . |

He states in the introductory verse (p. 2) that he made a
thorough study of the ष्क्‌ of Subhiiti, as also the comms.
Amarabhisya and Amaravarttika, etc:

Sei TA: . . . अमरमाप्यवार्तिकमुखान्‌ HAL बहूनादरात्‌ ।

Malli. confines himself to bringing out the meaning when
it differs from the interpretation of his predecessors. The
great task he undertakes is to supply the omissions in Ak.
This included collecting a list of words which were either
rarely used or had fallen into disuse, which Amara did not
mention.

14In one MS. (Aj) it is said in ए. 7 that Nrsimha, proficient in the
three Veda-s, was his paternal grandfather (nrsimho yatpitamahah) and
Tripurari his great grandfather. But in ए. 2 (12thin Aj) it is stated that
he was the son of Nrsimhasiirin (nysimhasiritanayah Srimallindtho muda). In
v. 8 (A) it is said that his father was called Naganathasudhi (क
ndganathasudhiyam namadmi janakam mama). But as MS. Ay in general, and
the introductory verses in particular, are somewhat unreliable and as the
colophon at the end of each Varga mentions Nrsimha as his father
(srivatsanysimhasirisutamallindthastiri. ..) we can take it that he was the son
of Nrsimha. Two or three colophons in A; mention the name of the
author as Mallikarjuna. He might have been thus affectionately addressed
by his friends or more probably it is a scribal error.
