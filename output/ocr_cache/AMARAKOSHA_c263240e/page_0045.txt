Lingayastrin was a scholar of profound learning. His
thorough acquaintance with the pafcamahakavya-s is seen from
the quotations in the Avyaya section of the Nandrthavarga
in Kanda Til. That his net is a wide one is proved from such
lines as

अथातो दरपू्णेमासौ व्यास्यास्यामः।

from the Srautasitra of Apastamba (I. 1). He also mentions
the grammarian Bhojaraja (p. 37). His eclectic mind includes
a considerable acquaintance with Buddhist philosophy. He
mentions the various temptations that stand in the way of the
spiritual aspirant:

the six special characteristics possessed by the enlightened
one, divyam caksuh divyam Srotram and the ten sources, of his

daka reveals his close acquaintance with political science
(p. 530).

Telugu equivalents are given by Siirin in the Vanausadhi-
varga, but unlike Malli. who gives Telugu words for all and
sundry, he does not approve of introducing other languages
in explaining Skt. words. In fact, Strin’s commentary is
very popular in regions where Tamil is the main language
spoken. It is only from verse 20 onwards in this section,
and that too only when absolutely necessary that he gives
Telugu meanings.

इह वृक्षलतौषधीनां नामान्यान्धरभाषेयैव वक्ष्यामः | (p. 229)

The Telugu words found in the various MSS. are not uniform;
pratti, patti and putt are given as Telugu equivalents of
karpasa (cotton). It may be inferred that the names vary
slightly according to the particular localities of the Telugu
country. MS. J. gives hatti, which is evidently a Kannada
word.
